# Cabinet 1.0.0

The first release without "alpha" in its name, and the first for three
platforms at once: iPhone, Apple TV, and now the Mac.

## Cabinet for Mac

A Mac has the desk, the disk and the chip the phone and the television do
not, so Cabinet for Mac is the television's app at arm's length, with the
systems only a desk machine can carry.

**PlayStation 2 and GameCube**, on the Mac alone, running PCSX2 and
Dolphin with their real recompilers, at full speed with audio. GameCube
gets its memory card synced to RomM, a save state slot and three picture
settings; PlayStation 2 gets its renderer remedy where you would look for
it.

**Dreamcast moves to the Mac.** On the phone and the television it never
reached full speed in heavy 3D, because those platforms do not allow the
kind of compilation the console's CPU needs, and a game that is not fully
playable is not offered. On the Mac the recompiler is on and it is. The
same rule now decides every core: fully playable, or not at all.

**The recompilers are on** for Nintendo DS and PlayStation too, which is
the reason the Mac exists.

**A source list, not tabs.** The library, your favourites and Downloaded
in a sidebar; a Settings window at Command-comma; menus that carry the
app's choices; the launch screen pushed in place with a back button; a
game screen and a control panel built for a pointer and a keyboard, or
your iPhone as the controller, as it already is for the television.

**Download All, per platform.** Right-click a platform, or use File,
and every game on it comes down in one queue, with the count and size
stated first and a status line at the foot of the sidebar while it runs.
Remove All Downloads and Show in Finder sit beside it; kept games live in
a Cabinet folder under Documents, laid out like your RomM library.

## Cabinet for iPhone

**Download All, for the phone.** The same one action per platform, from
the platform's menu or a long press on its tile. The files come down
through the system's own background downloader, over Wi-Fi only, so a
platform started at the table finishes with the phone locked in a
pocket; a queue the system quit the app in the middle of carries on at
the next launch. Progress lives where iOS shows it: the Dynamic Island,
the Lock Screen, and a card at the top of Home; a notification says when
it is done. On cellular it waits, and says so.

**A home screen widget**, in three sizes, showing what you played last,
your recents or your favourites, each cover a tap into its game. And
Spotlight: type three letters into the phone's own search and the game
is there, with its cover, and opens straight in.

**Your phone is the VMU.** While the Mac plays a Dreamcast game, the
phone in your hand shows the VMU's live screen, and the VMU minigames run
on the phone itself, through a twenty-third core.

**Experimental cores.** Nintendo 64 now sits behind a switch in Settings,
off by default: it runs, but speed varies by game, and the switch says so.

**Faster frames on both iPhone and Apple TV**, from a third rung on the
readback ladder that stops waiting for the GPU.

**And the fixes.** The app no longer freezes or gets killed at launch
with a large library of kept games. Removing a download in airplane mode
takes effect at once. Games the device cannot play stay on Home but
never in the hero. The pad stick's vertical axis now follows the
convention every other emulator uses.

## Cabinet for Apple TV

The phone-as-controller pairing on the television side; the experimental
cores switch; every settings switch in the page's own focus style; and
Home never offering to resume a game the television cannot play.

## What this release takes away

Dreamcast on iPhone and Apple TV. See above; it lives on the Mac now.

## Installing

iPhone and Apple TV: TestFlight, or the unsigned IPAs on this release.
Mac: the disk image on this release, signed and notarized; open it and
drag Cabinet to Applications.
